I am not a productivity guru. I do not wake up at 4 AM, I do not have a color-coded Notion dashboard, and I definitely do not meditate for an hour every morning. What I do have is a set of habits that, somehow, keep me moving forward as a developer and as a person. This is my routine — the one that is actually working for me right now — and I just want to share it honestly.

If you are a fellow dev, tech enthusiast, or just someone curious about what a "regular day" looks like for a developer working from home in the Philippines, this one is for you.

---

## Daily Code: The One-Line-of-Code Rule

Every single day, I push code to GitHub. That is the rule. It does not matter if it is one line, one commit, or a full feature — as long as something lands on my profile.

I am currently sitting on a **30+ day streak**, and honestly, it feels kind of ridiculous how motivating that little green square graph can be. Some days I work on personal projects, some days I touch a private company project that is connected to my GitHub account, and some days it is genuinely just a one-liner fix or a minor tweak.

The point is not to write a masterpiece every day. The point is to **show up**. There are mornings where I do not feel like coding at all — maybe I slept badly, maybe I am just not in the mood. But the streak keeps me honest. And more often than not, once I start typing, momentum carries me through.

What this habit is teaching me is simple: consistency beats intensity. You do not need to grind for eight hours straight. You just need to keep the habit alive.

---

## Monthly Blog: One Post at a Time

Alongside the daily code habit, I have committed to publishing at least **one blog post per month**. The goal is not to go viral or become a full-time content creator. It is to connect with others, share my thoughts, and document my journey as a developer.

Writing forces me to think more clearly. When I sit down to explain something I have been working on, I realize how much I actually understand versus how much I just "kind of know." It is a humbling process, and I think that is a good thing.

The audience is still small, and I am fine with that. Building a small footprint online takes time, and every post is a small deposit in that account. Someday, someone might stumble on one of these posts and find it useful. That is enough for now.

---

## Gaming as a Mental Reset

Let me get something out of the way: I play games. Mobile gacha games, MOBA titles, the occasional casual session. Nothing competitive, nothing ranked. I am not trying to climb leaderboards or go pro.

For me, gaming is a **mental reset**. When I have been staring at code for hours and my brain feels like mush, a short gaming session is the fastest way to decompress. It gives me a little dopamine boost, breaks the mental fatigue, and usually by the time I put the phone down, I am ready to focus again.

The key word here is **balance**. I keep it as leisure, not something that swallows my schedule. I know myself well enough to recognize when gaming is helping me recharge versus when it is just procrastination in disguise. Most of the time, it is the former. On the days it is the latter, I just move on and try again tomorrow.

---

## Workouts and the RunRito Project

I have an athletic side that surprises some people. After meals, I work out — minimum 15 minutes, often 30 minutes or more. It is not a structured program with a personal trainer. It is more like: finish eating, wait a bit, move my body. In-place jogging, push-ups, stretching, whatever feels right.

The hardest part is starting. There are days when I really do not feel like moving. But I have learned that once I begin, momentum takes over. The first five minutes are the hardest, and after that, I am usually in it.

I track my progress using **Strava**, and because I am a developer, I obviously had to build something around it. That is how **[RunRito](https://runrito.netlify.app)** was born.

The idea is straightforward: you log in with your Strava account, set a goal (like a certain number of kilometers in three months), and the app tells you how many kilometers you should be running per day to stay on track. You get graphs, progress visualization, and notifications about whether you are ahead or behind.

It is still a work in progress, but building it has been a great exercise in using code to support something I care about outside of work. There is something satisfying about debugging an API integration that directly affects your own fitness data.

## Fasting, Meals, and My Daily Drinks

I usually skip breakfast. Intermittent fasting has been working for me — I have seen enough research and YouTube deep-dives about how it can help regulate blood sugar and support overall wellness to feel good about it. I am not a nutritionist, and I do not remember all the technical details, but my body seems to like it, so I keep doing it.

Lunch is a **heavy home-cooked meal**. Dinner is also home-cooked. Filipino food, nothing fancy, just real meals made at home. I am grateful for that.

My drink routine is simple: in the morning, I wait about 30 minutes before having my first cup of coffee. At night, after my workout, I usually wind down with a cup of tea. These small rituals might sound insignificant, but they give my day a sense of rhythm and structure that I did not realize I needed until I started paying attention to them.

---

## Information Diet: AI Updates and Online Communities

I follow weekly AI updates, including a summary from **Perplexity AI** that helps me keep track of trends in the AI space, especially here in the Philippines. I sometimes joke that maybe one of these trends will reveal a "new hack to become a millionaire," but honestly, I treat it more as staying curious and informed.

I also regularly check the Reddit community **r/AtinAtinLang**, a Filipino "gatekeeping" subreddit where people share good deals, promos, and interesting finds. It is a fun community, and I have found some genuinely useful stuff through it.

That said, this kind of resource can be a double-edged sword. It is great for finding value, but you need self-control so you do not end up buying everything you see. I consider myself someone with enough restraint to treat it as a fun, useful resource rather than a spending trap. Most of the time, anyway.

---

## Netflix and Workouts: The Perfect Stack

During my workouts, I often watch **Netflix**. In-place jogging becomes a lot more bearable when you are half-watching a show while your legs do the work. Push-ups, stretching, bodyweight circuits — pairing them with something entertaining makes the whole experience more enjoyable.

This is basically **habit stacking** in its simplest form. I am not doing anything fancy. I am just making exercise feel less like a chore by attaching it to something I already enjoy. It works, and it keeps me consistent.

---

## Family and Relationships: Meals at Home and Nightly Calls

Since I work from home, I usually get to share lunch and dinner with my family. These are simple home-cooked meals, but eating together has become a small everyday ritual that keeps us connected. It is a chance to catch up, laugh, and feel grounded before and after a day of work and code.

Research shows that regular family meals can improve both physical and mental health, strengthen family bonds, and help everyone feel more supported. I do not think about the research when I am sitting at the table, but I can feel it in how these ordinary moments add up over time.

At night, I also set aside time to call my special someone. We talk about our day, share random stories, and most importantly, discuss our **future goals** together. Things like career plans, where we might live, financial goals, and the kind of life we want to build.

These conversations are not just romantic. They help us stay aligned, motivated, and committed. Talking openly about shared goals is known to increase relationship satisfaction, and I can feel that in how we support each other's dreams.

In the bigger picture, this mix of family mealtimes and nightly future-planning calls reminds me that my routine is not only about productivity or personal gains. It is also about **investing in relationships** and making sure my growth as a developer is connected to the people I care about.

## Looking Ahead: The Life I Am Building

Beyond just maintaining my current routine, I think a lot about where all of this is leading. The daily GitHub commits, monthly blogs, workouts, and AI reading are not random habits. They are small investments in the kind of developer and person I want to become.

Career-wise, I want to grow from "just shipping code" to someone who builds **meaningful products and systems**. That means improving my backend and API skills, experimenting more with AI tools, and maybe becoming known for a few solid open-source or personal projects instead of only hidden company work.

I also want my habits to scale with my ambitions. Better focus during work hours, fewer distractions, more intentional learning — deep dives into specific technologies instead of just scrolling tech news. Tiny, consistent habits are how I plan to build long-term productivity without burning out.

Financially and professionally, I imagine a future where I have more options. Maybe remote roles with better compensation, maybe my own product or SaaS, or collaborations with other devs on tools that actually help people. That is why I am slowly building my online presence through GitHub, this blog, and projects like RunRito.

During my nightly calls, I do not just talk about "us" as a couple. I also share my career plans, side project ideas, and long-term goals. Saying these things out loud helps me make them feel more concrete and keeps me accountable. It is like a mini check-in: am I moving toward the kind of life I keep describing, or am I just dreaming?

Overall, this routine is my way of stacking small, realistic actions so that future me — whether as a more senior engineer, a builder of my own products, or just a healthier and more grounded person — has more doors open. It is not perfect, but it is direction plus momentum. And for now, that is enough.

---

## Final Thoughts: Not Perfect, But Working

I am not going to pretend this routine is the most optimized or efficient way to spend a day. It probably is not. There are probably people who code more, work out more disciplined, and have more strategic career plans.

But right now, this combination of daily code commits, monthly blogs, casual gaming, consistent workouts, fasting, and curated information intake is working for me in a **realistic, sustainable way**. It does not require willpower mountains every morning. It just requires showing up, doing the small things, and trusting that they add up.

If you are a developer or tech person reading this, I would encourage you to think about your own "imperfect but working" routine. You do not need to overhaul your life. Just pick one small commitment — one line of code a day, a 15-minute workout, one blog post a month — and stick with it for a while. See what happens.

The compound effect of small, consistent habits is real. You just have to be patient enough to let it work.

As for me, I will keep showing up. One commit, one post, one workout, one cup of coffee at a time. And maybe, someday, I will look back at this routine and laugh at how simple it was. But simple is not the same as ineffective. Sometimes, simple is exactly what works.
